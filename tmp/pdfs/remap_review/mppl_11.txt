E. Improvements on Skeleton-Based Models
As a plug-and-play enhancement, our method can improve
the recognition performance of any mainstream skeleton-based
model. Therefore, we evaluate representative GCN-based and
attention-based models. MPPL significantly improves the per-
formance of all single-modality models (Table IX). Notably,
a weaker baseline like ST-GCN exhibits the largest gains,
while stronger models (e.g., AGMS-GCN, SelfGCN) still
gain 1.5%–4%. Overall, MPPL enhances generalization and
robustness, demonstrating the effectiveness of multimodal
progressive prompt learning. We further compare the accuracy
of MPPL and the skeleton encoder on similar categories (Fig.
10). The skeleton encoder alone (a) struggles with similar hand
motions (0.68–0.69 accuracy for “make OK sign” and “make
victory sign”). Adding ECMA or PRB individually improves
accuracy, while the full MPPL framework achieves 95.10%
on X-Set and significantly reduces confusion, confirming its
effectiveness in enhancing action recognition performance and
robustness.
F . Analysis of Complexity and Limitations
We analyze the model complexity of MPPL alongside
several mainstream methods (Table X). Multimodal methods
typically require more parameters, up to 171.98M. MPPL
ranks third in size among the five methods but achieves
the highest accuracy. LMMs like MiniGPT-v2/4 and CLIP
consume substantial resources. Overall, MPPL balances effi-
ciency and accuracy, achieving top performance with moderate
parameters and FLOPs. Despite its strong performance, MPPL
has limitations. The large size and computational cost hinder
deployment in resource-constrained scenarios. Additionally,
MPPL exhibits limited capability in recognizing two-person
interaction actions, which may be due to the insufficient ability
of MM-Gen to interpret dual-subject images (see Fig. 11
and 12). Future work will focus on lightweight architectures,
efficient inference, and improved multimodal generation to
enhance performance in diverse scenarios.
V. CONCLUSION
We propose a novel Multi-modality Progressive Prompt
Learning (MPPL) framework that leverages large multimodal
models to enrich skeleton-based action representations with
environmental appearance and fine-grained semantic informa-
tion, enabling efficient and accurate human action recognition.
MPPL offers three key advantages. First, the Multimodal
Generation (MM-Gen) module generates vision and language
modalities from video frames, addressing the lack of envi-
ronmental and fine-grained cues in skeleton features. Second,
the Entropy-based Cross-Modal Alignment (ECMA) module
effectively suppresses irrelevant noise in vision and language
features, achieving bidirectional cross-modal alignment. Third,
the Prompt Refinement Branch (PRB) introduces language-
aware cues that complement the semantic descriptions in
ECMA, directly participating in the decision process through
adaptive refinement and enhancement of classification features.
Experiments on four mainstream datasets demonstrate that
MPPL achieves competitive performance across both single-
modal and multimodal methods.
