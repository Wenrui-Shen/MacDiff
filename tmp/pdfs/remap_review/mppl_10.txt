To analyze entropy distributions across different sample
types, we randomly sample instances from simple actions
(single-person with low interaction) and complex actions (two-
person or high interaction), and visualize the results using box-
plots, as shown in Fig. 8. No significant difference is observed
between the two groups in either modality (Vision:p= 0.725,
Language:p= 0.662), indicating that higher entropy is not
inherently caused by action complexity. Although complex
actions exhibit slightly larger IQRs, suggesting greater vari-
ability, their median entropy remains comparable to that of
simple actions. Notably, high-entropy outliers appear in both
groups and are often associated with noisy or ambiguous
samples (e.g., background clutter or occlusion), implying that
extreme entropy values mainly reflect uncertainty rather than
informative complexity.

We further compare different vision and language encoders
to verify the adaptability and robustness of the ECMA module
(Table VII). The results show that after introducing ECMA,
performance improves significantly across all setups, whether
using a combination of ResNet50, Inception-V3, and BERT,
or pre-trained CLIP models such as ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, and
ViT-L/14, stabilizing around 95.0%. The best performance
of 95.1% is achieved with Inception-V3 + BERT combined
with CLIP (ViT-L/14). These results demonstrate that ECMA
effectively suppresses semantic noise and enhances cross-
modal alignment across different encoders, exhibiting strong
versatility and robustness.
