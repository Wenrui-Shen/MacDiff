clude composite RGB images, ROI frames, and raw frames,
where ROI frames refer to ROI-extracted frames that are
independently encoded at the frame level. Due to the input
constraints of LMMs, ROI frames and raw frames are not used
for language generation. As shown in Table V, raw frames
achieve the lowest performance, indicating that background in-
terference and fragmented representations hinder cross-modal
alignment and motion modeling. ROI frames consistently
outperform raw frames, showing the benefit of removing
background noise. Composite RGB images achieve the best
performance, suggesting that spatially stitching ROI-extracted
frames provides a more coherent representation than inde-
pendently encoding frames followed by feature aggregation.
Regarding sampling strategies, random sampling performs the
worst due to the lack of temporal structure. Joint velocity prior-
itizes frames with high instantaneous joint speed, while motion
magnitude selects frames with large global pose displacement.
Both improve accuracy by focusing on salient motion but may
overlook transitional stages, leading to slightly higher PPL. In
contrast, uniform sampling selects frames at equal temporal
intervals, ensuring balanced temporal coverage and achieving
the best overall performance with the lowest PPL, highest
accuracy, and minimal computational complexity. Its moderate
SB score indicates a balanced trade-off between linguistic
diversity and semantic alignment. Therefore, uniform sampling
on composite RGB images is adopted as the default setting in
MM-Gen.
