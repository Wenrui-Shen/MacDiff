IV. EXPERIMENTS
We conducted extensive comparative experiments, ablation
studies, and specialized analyses to comprehensively evaluate
the effectiveness of the proposed method on four mainstream
datasets: NTU-RGB+D 60 [14] / 120 [25], Kinetics-skeleton
[26], and Northwestern-UCLA [27].
A. Datasets
1) NTU-RGB+D [14]:The NTU-RGB+D 60 dataset con-
tains 60 action classes with 56,880 video samples collected
from 40 subjects and three camera views, providing RGB,
depth, infrared, and 25-joint skeleton modalities. We evaluate
action recognition performance using classification accuracy
across all datasets, defined as the ratio of correctly predicted
samples to the total number of samples. For NTU-RGB+D 60,
two standard evaluation protocols are adopted: Cross-Subject
(X-Sub) with 40,320 training and 16,560 testing samples, and
Cross-View (X-View) with 37,920 training (cameras 2–3) and
18,960 testing (camera 1) samples.
2) NTU-RGB+D 120 [25]:The NTU-RGB+D 120 dataset
expands NTU-RGB+D 60 to 120 action classes with 114,480
videos from 106 subjects captured by multiple Kinect V2
cameras. The evaluation metrics employ two official proto-
cols: Cross-Subject (X-Sub), with 63,026 training and 50,922
testing samples, and Cross-Setup (X-Set), with 59,477 training
and 54,471 testing samples.
3) Kinetics-skeleton [26]:This dataset contains 300,000
video clips from Kinetics-400 with 18-joint skeletons extracted
using OpenPose, providing a large and challenging benchmark
for action recognition. Following common practice, we report
Top-1/-5 accuracy as the evaluation metrics for action classi-
fication.
4) Northwestern-UCLA [27]:The N-UCLA dataset in-
cludes 1,494 skeletons of 10 classes from 10 subjects, captured
by three cameras. Following the NTU X-View protocol, cam-
eras 1–2 are for training and camera 3 is for testing. Due to
the relatively small scale of this dataset, only Top-1 accuracy
is reported.
5) Micro-action dataset:Micro-Action 52 [28] is a large-
scale micro-action dataset capturing unconscious human be-
haviors through professional interviews. The dataset com-
prises 52 categories and contains 22,422 samples. In addition,
we also evaluate our method on iMiGUE [29], a micro-
gesture dataset collected from post-competition athlete inter-
views, consisting of 32 upper-body gesture categories, totaling
18,232 samples. Following standard practice, we evaluate
these datasets using Top-1/-5 accuracy.
B. Implementation detail
All experiments are conducted on a server with four
NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs using PyTorch. In the MM-Gen
module, MiniGPT-V2 and MiniGPT-4 generate images and
language descriptions, respectively. The ECMA module uses
pre-trained CLIP [11] ViT-L/14 vision and text encoders, and
the PRB employs CLIP’s text encoder. The models are trained
with SGD (momentum 0.9, weight decay 0.0005) for 150

C. Comparison with the state-of-the-art
We conduct a comprehensive comparison with state-of-
the-art action recognition methods, including both skeleton-
based single-modal and multimodal methods, as shown in
Tables I, II, and III. On the NTU-RGB+D 60/120 dataset,
our method achieves competitive performance. Multimodal
approaches generally outperform single-modal methods due
to the complementary information across modalities.
On the NTU-RGB+D 60, our method outperforms the latest
single-modal methods AGMS-GCN (0.2%/0.1%), SelfGCN
(1.0%/1.3%), and AG-HGNN (1.0%/0.7%) under the X-Sub
and X-View settings, respectively. From the attention heatmap
of the action “writing”, we observe that MPPL (S+R+L)
