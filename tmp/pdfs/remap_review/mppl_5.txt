E. Multimodal collaborative learning
In multimodal collaborative learning, combining image–text
features with skeleton features enhances the skeleton encoder’s
ability to capture complex semantics. Thus, effective alignment
and fusion across these three modalities is crucial. Building
on the robust cross-modal alignment achieved by the ECMA
module, we introduce a multimodal collaborative learning
strategy to exploit the complementary strengths of vision and
language in skeleton representation. Specifically, we employ
the InfoNCE loss [23] to align the remapped vision features
f rv and language featuresf rl with the skeleton featuresf s
in a pairwise manner. This alignment encourages skeleton
representations to move closer to both vision and language
modalities, enhancing cross-modal semantic consistency. The
multimodal alignment loss is defined as:
Lrv↔rl =L rv→rl +L rl→rv ,(9)
Ls↔rv =L s→rv +L rv→s,(10)
Ls↔rl =L s→rl +L rl→s.(11)
Lrv↔rl reinforces vision–language collaboration,L s↔rv
transfers visual cues to skeletons, andL s↔rl injects language
semantics. Overall, we use cross-entropy loss as the main
classification objective and add these multimodal alignment
losses as auxiliary supervision. The final training loss is
defined as:
Lmppl =Lcls +λ 1(Ls↔rv +L s↔rv)+

whereλ 1,λ 2, andλ 3 are hyperparameters, set to 0.7, 0.4, and
0.5, respectively.
F . Refinement prompt learning
Multimodal collaborative learning enhances the skeleton
encoder’s capacity to model cross-modal features. However,
since auxiliary modalities do not directly participate in the
classification stage or explicitly align with category labels,
their contribution to discriminative decision-making remains
limited. To further exploit cross-modal semantic information
for classification, we introduce a Prompt Refinement Branch
(PRB) that bridges skeleton representations with category text
embeddings. Inspired by CoOp [24], PRB performs similarity-
based classification by comparing skeleton features with clas-
sification weights generated by the text encoder. After multi-
